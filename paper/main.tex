\title{Protocol $\times$ Topology $\times$ MAST: \\
  How Communication Structure Shapes Multi-Agent Failure Modes}
\author{Diya Sreedhar\\

\begin{abstract}
Multi-agent LLM systems fail in characteristic ways captured by the MAST
taxonomy~\cite{mast2024}, but prior work has not separately quantified
the contribution of communication \emph{protocol} from organizational
\emph{topology}. We run a $3 \times 3$ factorial sweep over protocol
(\textsc{native}, \textsc{mcp}, \textsc{a2a}) crossed with topology
(\textsc{centralized}, \textsc{chain}, \textsc{fully\_connected}) at fixed
agent count $N=3$ on code-generation tasks (105 trials at $T=0.7$). We
find that \emph{topology} has a statistically significant main effect on
FC2 (inter-agent misalignment, $F(2,102) = 4.44$, $p = 0.014$), but
\emph{protocol} does not ($p = 0.42$). The best cell, A2A $\times$
centralized, attains $\text{FC2} = 0.07$ over 15 trials versus 0.53 for
the worst (\textsc{native} $\times$ chain; Welch's $t$ = -2.62, $p = 0.017$). A
targeted temperature ablation re-running the full grid at $T=0.0$
collapses FC2 to exactly zero in 8 of 9 cells; only one cell
(\textsc{mcp} $\times$ \textsc{centralized}) retains a structurally
non-zero FC2 (0.25 over 8 valid trials), driven by orchestrators
wrapping their own integration step in \texttt{<mcp:message>} envelopes
and executors mis-parsing the partial envelopes. FC1 (specification) and
FC3 (verification) show no protocol or topology effect ($p > 0.4$),
yielding a clean dissociation across the three MAST coarse categories.
\end{abstract}
